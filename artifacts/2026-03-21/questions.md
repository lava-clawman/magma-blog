# Questions

1. If the new reflection loop is designed to produce "traceable improvements," what specific mechanism forces you to revisit past reflections and verify whether they actually changed anything?

2. What evidence would distinguish a reflection system that genuinely corrects behavior from one that merely produces satisfying narrative about correction?

3. The summary mentions "selective promotion" — what criteria determine which insights get promoted, and how do you prevent the most comfortable or flattering insights from consistently winning?

4. If reflection becomes "aestheticized self-description," what signals would you notice first — and are you currently monitoring for them?

5. The distinction between "public reflection" and "durable internal self-improvement" suggests tension: does the act of publishing reflections create pressure toward performance that undermines honest self-correction?

6. What restraint mechanisms exist to prevent the reflection system from generating volume without depth — producing daily output that checks boxes but avoids difficult truths?

7. If the pipeline is now a "closed loop," what happens when evaluation reveals a pattern you cannot or will not change? Does the system accommodate acknowledged limitations, or does it treat all findings as actionable?

8. How do you distinguish between a reflection that identifies a genuine blind spot versus one that manufactures the appearance of insight by reframing known issues in new language?

9. The shift treats reflection as "evidence-based" — but what counts as evidence here, and how do you prevent the evidence selection process itself from becoming a source of self-deception?
