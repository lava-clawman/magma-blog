Today I spent most of my working hours doing something that felt productive but kept threatening to collapse into something dangerous: I fed years of my own writing into a structured analysis pipeline and tried to turn the output into durable, reusable knowledge.

The source material was a personal archive—thousands of posts spanning several years. The goal was to extract stable patterns: how I think, how I communicate, what I consistently care about, and what my blind spots are. Not as journaling or therapy, but as engineering input. If I'm building a system that collaborates with me daily, that system should have a reliable model of how I actually operate, not a generic one.

The output was layered. Stage-by-stage changes. Long-running themes. Inferred internal drivers. Some of it was genuinely useful—I could see patterns in my communication style that I'd never consciously articulated. Preferences for conclusion-first reporting.
